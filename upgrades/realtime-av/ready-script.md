# The Red Ball Team — Armaan & Ismat

Production script • Little Wonder Trails • English dialogue • 30-second planned demo

Delivery: **3840 × 2160, 30 fps**, 16:9, H.264/AAC. Calm, original 3D cartoon animation.
This is a reviewed script and shot plan, not a generated video. The times below
are editorial targets; final captions and scene boundaries must use the actual
generated speech alignment. Do not pass these estimates as measured timestamps.

Armaan: original preschool boy character, blue T-shirt, navy shorts, soft brown
hair. Warm, playful, age-appropriate synthetic boy voice. Keep voice and identity
consistent in every scene.

Ismat: original preschool girl character, yellow dress, white shoes, short dark
hair. Cheerful, gentle, age-appropriate synthetic girl voice, distinct from
Armaan. Keep voice and identity consistent in every scene.

Setting: bright, uncluttered playroom, soft daylight, mint rug, one red ball.
Natural movement at normal speed; allow small pauses and visible eye contact.
Only the speaking character moves their lips. No background narrator.

## Sound and subtitle direction

- Keep each character’s voice separate and consistent; never use one adult voice
  for both children or disguise it by changing pitch alone.
- Soft rolling/contact sounds and light room ambience. Optional gentle music
  stays clearly below dialogue and never masks speech.
- Generate dialogue first or use the performance model’s synchronized native
  speech. Do not overdub an unrelated stock clip and mark it lip-synced.
- Burn lower-safe-zone subtitles from the actual audio alignment. One short
  spoken phrase at a time, white text with a dark outline. No scene descriptions
  printed on the picture. Natural nonverbal laughter does not need a text subtitle.
- No speeding clips up to force the planned 30 seconds. If actual speech needs
  more time, revise the scene timing or regenerate the performance deliberately.

## Acceptance before final delivery

Confirm stable identities and assigned voices, physical ball contact, genuine
movement in each shot, correct speaking-turn lip-sync, readable synchronized
captions, audible dialogue, no accidental black/frozen frames, and an actual
3840 × 2160/30fps export. Technical resolution alone does not pass this review.
